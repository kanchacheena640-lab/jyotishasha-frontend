# Jyotishasha Editorial Rules — Confident, Positive, Accurate

**Status:** Permanent — applies to all new content and every content review
**Applies to:** astrology articles, hub pages, free tools (copy and results), report product pages,
report-generation prompts and PDFs, focused-report questions/descriptions, CTAs, metadata, app copy —
English and Hindi alike.
**Relationship to other rules:** complements `CONTENT_RULES.md` (frozen). `CONTENT_RULES.md` keeps
governing accuracy, safety and disclosures (§1.3 Never Claim Certainty, §3.5 Disclaimer, §11 claim
calibration). This document governs **how** those standards are expressed. If the two ever seem to
conflict, accuracy and legally required disclosure win; the wording is then made as positive as accuracy
allows.

---

## 1. The rule

Write in a **confident, positive, informative and solution-oriented** voice. Tell the reader what their
chart shows and what they can do next. Express likelihood through precise, constructive wording — not
through repeated negations, apologies or unsolicited limitation statements.

Never trade accuracy for confidence: no exaggerated certainty, fabricated claims or misleading guarantees.

## 2. Principles

1. **Lead with what the chart shows.** Open answers, cards and CTAs with the insight ("The 7th lord's
   Dasha is the main marriage window"), not with what astrology cannot do.
2. **Frame likelihood positively and precisely.** Use "supportive period", "strongest window",
   "indicates", "points to", "most active when". The probabilistic nature of astrology is carried by these
   words; it does not need a separate denial in every paragraph.
3. **No unsolicited limitation statements.** Do not append a limitation the reader did not ask about to
   answers, hero text, bullets, CTAs, tool results or product copy. Examples to avoid (EN/HI):
   - "no guaranteed date", "not a fixed date", "not a guarantee", "we cannot predict…",
     "astrology cannot tell you…", "results may not be accurate"
   - "कोई पक्की तारीख नहीं", "कोई गारंटी नहीं", "निश्चित नहीं कहा जा सकता", "ज्योतिष यह नहीं बता सकता"
4. **Answer limitation questions when they are asked — once, factually, and constructively.** If the
   reader's own question is about exactness (e.g. FAQ "Can astrology predict my exact wedding date?"),
   answer it directly in that place, and pair it with what astrology *does* provide:
   "Astrology identifies the periods when marriage is most strongly supported; the exact date is chosen
   with a Muhurat." Do not repeat that answer elsewhere on the page.
5. **Be solution-oriented.** Turn every constraint into a next step: a section, a free tool, a report, a
   Muhurat page. "Your current Dasha builds the foundation; the next supportive window begins when…"
   is better than "this period is not favourable".
6. **No fear and no false hope.** Challenging placements are described with their constructive
   expression and what helps; favourable placements are described without promising outcomes
   (see `CONTENT_RULES.md` §1.3–1.4).
7. **Say it once.** A necessary qualification appears once, in the right place — never as a refrain in
   every block of the same page.

## 3. What stays (required, not optional)

These are not "limitation statements" and must be preserved where they genuinely apply:

- Disclaimers required by `CONTENT_RULES.md` §3.5 (timing of major life events, health, significant
  decisions) — brief, calm, once, at the end of the page.
- Health, legal and financial boundaries: no diagnosis, no legal or investment advice; astrology is one
  input alongside professional advice where stakes are high.
- Purchase and policy facts: price, what the report contains, delivery method, refund/terms links.
- Factual qualifications that change meaning (e.g. "within the next five years" for a ranked window,
  "for two people: both birth details needed").
- Accurate product scope: a report page names only what the generator actually writes.

## 4. What is never allowed

- Certainty or guarantee language: "guaranteed", "100%", "will definitely", "is certain to", "पक्का होगा",
  "गारंटी है".
- Specific event dates or ages presented as predictions.
- Fabricated statistics, testimonials, ratings, reviews, credentials or classical sources.
- Claims that a report, tool or remedy contains or achieves something it does not.

## 5. Before → after

| Before (avoid) | After (use) |
|---|---|
| "The result is a likely timing window — not a guaranteed date." | "Together these show the periods when marriage is most strongly supported in your chart." |
| "इससे विवाह की संभावित समय-अवधि का संकेत मिलता है — कोई पक्की तारीख नहीं।" | "इन्हें साथ देखकर आपकी कुंडली में विवाह के सबसे अनुकूल समय की पहचान की जाती है।" |
| "Astrology cannot tell you exactly when you will marry, but…" | "Your chart shows when marriage is most strongly supported:" |
| "This period is not good for marriage." | "This period builds the groundwork; the next supportive window opens with …" |
| "Results are not guaranteed." (on a CTA) | *(remove; keep any required disclaimer once at the page end)* |

## 6. Review checklist

- [ ] Opening sentence of every answer, card and CTA states an insight or action.
- [ ] No unsolicited limitation phrases (§2.3) in answers, hero, bullets, CTAs, tool results, product copy.
- [ ] Any exactness/limitation question is answered once, where asked, with what astrology does provide.
- [ ] No certainty, guarantee, fabricated or misleading claims (§4).
- [ ] Required disclaimers and factual qualifications (§3) are present, brief and not repeated.
- [ ] English and Hindi versions say the same thing with the same confidence.
- [ ] Tests that pin copy assert the intent (no guarantees, accurate scope), not a limitation phrase.

## 7. Application

- **New content and reviews:** apply this document from now on, in English and Hindi.
- **Existing content:** changed only in approved, scoped edits (one page or product at a time), so
  each change is reviewed with its snapshot and copy tests.
